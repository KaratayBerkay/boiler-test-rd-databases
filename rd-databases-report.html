Engines and bulk load

Feature probes

DDL/DML constructs tried verbatim on each engine (dialect-adjusted syntax). Supported means the statement ran and, where it was meant to fail (constraints, timeouts), failed with the expected error.

Optimisation experiments

Each experiment times a query, applies one change (index, rewrite, statistics, batching, prepared statements), and times it again. Speed-up is p50 of the first variant divided by p50 of the best variant.

Connections

Connect latency includes one round trip; storms open N connections at once and record the error at the limit; throughput uses one forked process per connection running primary-key lookups.

Replication and failover

Visibility lag: insert on the primary, poll the replica until the row appears. Failover: docker kill the primary, promote, time until the first successful write.

Backup and recovery drills

Every strategy is executed, not described: back up into the stack's shared /backups volume, restore into a second database, a scratch container or in place, compare a fingerprint of all seven tables with the source (verified), and bring back a 1000-row probe table written after the full backup and deleted after the recovery point (PITR / probe). Sizes are bytes on disk.

Logging

Slow-query sink with a 100 ms threshold, a tagged slow (~300 ms) and fast statement to prove capture and filtering, an audit-trail proof (tagged DDL), and the throughput cost of logging every statement (8 processes doing primary-key lookups for 4 s; differences under ~10 % are run-to-run noise). Every container logs through the Docker json-file driver with rotation (50 MB × 3, compressed).

Method

One 28-core host, every container on the same machine (Docker Compose v5). Stacks run one at a time. Timings use monotonic clocks; results checksummed per query; plans stored next to timings under results/<engine>/plans/. Harness: harness/rdlab; regenerate this page with scripts/build-report.py.
